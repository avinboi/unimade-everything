How to read the numbers

Revenue = what students pay (GMV). That's the "net transaction value" rule from S16, so we count no MRP and no inflated list prices. Unimade keeps about 26% of it after paying partners and payment fees. That 26% is our gross margin, so break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ 0.261 = 3.83.
Amber = assumption or estimate. Market prices come from public listings (sources on p. 18). Campus numbers, conversion rates and CPCs are our own planning guesses. The ₹2,000 Meta and ₹2,000 Google pilots in December (Checkpoint 4) are where those guesses get replaced by real numbers.
the one-line plan
Win one campus completely before spending on anyone outside it.
With 1,500 students in a few buildings, posters, creators and WhatsApp groups reach people more cheaply than ads do. Ads are only a small part of the plan, and they have to beat break-even to keep their budget.

How Unimade makes money

Unimade is a managed student marketplace. A student books and pays Unimade one clear price. Unimade gives the job to a verified local partner (a laundromat, a cleaner or a home kitchen), pays them a fixed bulk rate every week, and keeps the difference. The student never deals with five vendors, and the partner never has to find students.


Student
books on WhatsApp or the site, pays by UPI, drops the bag at the hostel desk

Partner

Why a spread, and not a commission or our own laundry

No machines, no staff.
Students can't fund a laundromat. Partners already own the capacity, and most of it sits idle on weekdays.
We control the price.
Our site promises "not 17 hidden fees". Under a commission, the partner sets the price. With a spread, the student sees one fixed number.
Batching is the moat.
One desk pickup for 30 students is cheaper for the partner, which is why they'll accept ₹45/kg (rate to negotiate).

Why these numbers

₹69/kg wash & fold. That sits in the middle of the ₹59–75/kg Gurugram range and has no 5 kg minimum (we ask for 4 kg). The student wins on convenience, since we pick up from the hostel desk. The ₹499 Pass (8 kg, so ₹62/kg) copies the market's 10–20% prepaid discount and gives us cash upfront.
₹299 room clean. Urban Company charges ₹799 for a bedroom. A hostel room is smaller, and one cleaner can do 6 rooms in the same building in a morning: 6 × ₹210 = ₹1,260 for about 4.5 hours, which a local cleaner will take (to confirm).
Tiffin at only 10%. Food margins are thin, and kitchens won't give more. Tiffin is here because it keeps students opening the WhatsApp chat every day, not because it earns.
What we refuse to do. No delivery fee, no surge pricing, no ₹50 "service fee" (that was in our earlier newsletter idea). On a ₹276 laundry order, a ₹50 fee is an 18% price hike, and students would notice.

Why this plan, in one page

Brief: 250–400 words on the reasoning: which channels, which audience, what outcomes, backed by metrics and benchmarks. This note: 338 words.

Why these channels. Unimade launches on one campus of about 1,500 students, roughly 1,000 of whom live in hostels or PGs. That changes how media works. We can reach the whole market by walking through a few buildings, so the cheapest reach isn't an ad. It's a poster by the laundry room, a creator who lives two doors down, and a WhatsApp group everyone already reads. So only 47% of our ₹30,000 goes to paid media (the course example used 60%), and 23% goes to WhatsApp, email and referral credits. Their job is the second order, not the first.

Who we're targeting. First- and second-year students living away from home for the first time. They're busy, they have exams on a fixed calendar, and their laundry happens on Sunday. Their problem is time, not money: about 8 hours a month. So we don't sell cheap laundry. Our one promise is "Get your Sunday back."

Why laundry leads. It's weekly, so it becomes a habit. It's visible, since bags at the hostel desk advertise for free. Cleaning and tiffin then use the same desk and the same WhatsApp chat, so the platform grows without paying again to acquire anyone.

What we expect. 360 paying students in 90 days (36% of residents) and ₹3.97 L in bookings, of which Unimade keeps about ₹1.04 L at a 26% blended margin. Break-even ROAS is 1 ÷ 0.261 = 3.83. We target paid ROAS of at least 5.0 and MER of at least 8. Meta's first-order ROAS is just 1.8, so ads only pay if students come back. That's why our north star is repeat pickups per week. Blended CAC of ₹83 against a 12-month LTV of ₹809 on margin gives about 9.7:1.

Benchmarks we used. Gurugram wash-and-fold at ₹59–75/kg (LBB); Urban Company at ₹799 for a bedroom and ₹579 for a bathroom; and the course's 65% margin gate, break-even ROAS and 60/30/10 rules. Every number is an assumption until the Week-1 survey and the live ₹4,000 Meta and Google pilot replace it.

in one breath
Own one campus. Win the first order cheaply, using people more than ads. Earn the money back on the second, third and tenth orders. Spend more only where ROAS beats 3.83.

ATL idea: “Weigh Your Week”

Brief: an Above The Line activation idea + a budget split sheet following the 60/30/10 rule.
single-minded proposition
Get your Sunday back.
Unimade helps hostel students skip laundry day. The headline is 4 words, one promise, one audience, and readable in 3 seconds.

The activation

For three days in the second week of Term 2 (12–14 Jan), we set up a hanging luggage scale and a 6-foot pile of (clean, borrowed) laundry bags at the busiest spot on campus, the mess entrance (needs admin permission).

"Sunday's coming. Want us to take the pile this Thursday? Reply 1 and drop the bag at the desk. STOP to opt out."

Naming rule
All lowercase, no spaces, hyphens only. Source = where the click came from (instagram, google, whatsapp, poster-h1-lift). Medium = cpc / social / message / offline. One campaign name per push.
Why repeat pickups, not purchases?
GA defaults to purchases. But we lose money on a first order after ad costs (first-order ROAS 1.8), and we earn on the second. A habit business should count the habit.
Moment of truth
Every Monday we check GA's purchases against our UPI settlements and the WhatsApp labels. If they disagree, the payment records win.
