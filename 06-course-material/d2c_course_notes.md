## Session 1 — Business Idea & Project Kickoff
**Core question:** "What makes a business idea strong enough to turn into a real venture?"

**Why ideas fail:** solution-first thinking; "I think people will want this" ("A hunch is not evidence"); "Most ideas die because nobody was hurting enough to pay — not because the product was badly built."

**Real vs assumed problem:**
- *Real* = something you or someone you know "has personally paid to solve, or actively complained about."
- *Assumed* = sounds logical but nobody has ever paid to fix it.
- **Test:** "Can you name one specific person who has this problem today?"

**Framework — Problem-Solution-Customer sentence:**
> "For [target customer] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [alternative], [differentiation]."
Short version (Steve Blank): **"We help X do Y by doing Z."** Rule: "Every word must be specific. No 'everyone.'"
- Breww 47 example: "For office-goers who want good coffee without the café wait, Breww 47 is a quick-service coffee outlet that delivers café-quality coffee in minutes. Unlike a sit-down café, Breww 47 is built for speed at peak hours — not ambience." / "We help office-goers get great coffee fast by running quick-service outlets built for peak-hour speed." (grounded in the 71–74% 11am–4pm data)

**Idea Validation Quick Test:** (1) *Search Test* — do people already complain about it or pay to solve it online? (2) *3-Person Test* — describe the **problem, not the solution**, to 3 people outside class; do they recognise it?

**Elevator pitch structure (60 seconds, strict):** Hook → Problem → Solution → Ask.

**Idea Generation Worksheet:** Segment → Problem → Current Alternative → Your Fix. Common traps: "Everyone" as the segment; leaving the current-alternative field blank.

**3-Question Filter:** *Who hurts?* (a specific, named customer) / *Why now?* (what changed) / *Why you?* (unfair advantage).

**Reflection questions:** Have you personally felt the problem? Would family or friends pay today? "What is the smallest version of this idea you could test in 2 weeks?"

**Deliverables:** 1-slide elevator pitch + 1-line problem statement + named target customer. Post-class: the pitch + completed Idea Generation Worksheet. Set **3 measurable goals** for the course project.
Pre-class reference: YC-style one-liners (Airbnb "Book accommodations around the world", Stripe "Economic infrastructure for the internet", Dropbox, Reddit, DoorDash "Restaurant delivery").

## Session 2 — Lean Canvas / Business Model Template
**Why a canvas rather than a 40-page plan:** "A plan documents assumptions as if they were already facts." A canvas exposes them, since "every box is a guess you can go test this week." "Speed to test beats completeness": you can rewrite a canvas in 20 minutes, while a formal plan takes 3 weeks.

**The 9 boxes and what each must contain:**
| Box | Instruction |
|---|---|
| Problem | Top 3 problems, ranked by how much they hurt |
| Existing Alternatives | What the customer does instead today — "name the real competitor" |
| Solution | Top 3 features mapped 1:1 to the top 3 problems, "no extras yet" |
| Unique Value Proposition | One sentence on why you're different — not a generic promise |
| Customer Segments | Specific early-adopter segment, not "everyone who needs X" |
| Channels | How customers discover and buy — "be specific, not 'social media'" |
| Revenue Streams | Pricing model + expected price point — "a real number, not a placeholder" |
| Cost Structure | Biggest cost line items — real estimate, even if rough |
| Key Metrics | (e.g. Breww 47: daily orders, attach rate, % of orders in peak window) |

**Framework — "The 9 Box Reality Check":** "Every box must carry a real number — not a placeholder like 'TBD' or 'a lot.'" Placeholder: "We'll get a lot of customers." Real: "450 orders/month from 3 nearby office parks." "If a box has no real number, that's this week's homework."

**Breww 47 canvas:** Segment = office-goers and urban professionals near outlets; Problem = long café queues, bad coffee at home, no fast option in the work-hour rush; Alternatives = sit-down cafés (slow), instant coffee, vending machines; UVP = "Café-quality coffee in minutes — built for speed, not ambience"; Solution = quick-service outlets tuned to the 11am–4pm rush; Key Metrics = daily orders, attach rate, % of orders in peak window; Channels = outlet visibility, peak-hour social content, word of mouth; Costs = outlet rent and staff, beans and milk, packaging; Revenue = per-cup sales + loyalty for repeat office customers.

**Peer-review red flags:** segment says "everyone"; revenue has no number; UVP "could apply to any competitor."

**Risk questions:** Which box is hardest *for your category*? For a physical product, is manufacturing the real constraint? Would a customer "with money already in hand" behave the way your Channels box assumes?

**Deliverables:** Problem, Solution, Channels, Revenue required in class. Post-class: all 9 boxes, with **at least one real number in Revenue and in Cost**. (The S2 price and COGS are reused for the S16 margin gate and S18 break-even ROAS.)

## Session 3 — Naming, Domain Search & Logo
**3 naming techniques (with Indian D2C examples):**
- **Descriptive** — names the function; builds instant clarity and trust: *Minimalist, The Whole Truth, SuperBottoms*.
- **Invented** — made-up word; distinctive and ownable: *Zingavita, Antinorm, 82°E*.
- **Metaphor** — borrows meaning; builds feeling and lifestyle association: *Rage Coffee, Bewakoof, Snitch*.
- Category guidance: protein and skincare reward **descriptive** names; apparel and coffee reward **evocative** ones. Real names often **blend** techniques. Breww 47 = descriptive root "Brew" + invented spelling (extra w) + a number for intrigue.

**Names that stick:** short and easy to say; hint at the category or feeling; "survives being said over a noisy call." **Names that fade:** hard to spell after hearing once; generic; "tries to say everything at once."

**Framework — Say It, Spell It, Search It** (must clear all three):
1. *Say It* — can someone hear it once and say it back?
2. *Spell It* — can someone type it correctly after only hearing it?
3. *Search It* — are the domain and handle available?

**Availability check:** domain on Namecheap or GoDaddy (.com or .in); Instagram username search ("a great name with no available handle creates confusion").

**Logo principles for non-designers:** *Simplicity* (works as a thumbnail icon); *Scalability* (legible at favicon and billboard size, so test both); **1–2 colours max** ("fewer colors read as more premium"). Build the first draft in Canva and test it at icon size.

**Name questions:** Will the name still make sense if you expand into adjacent products in 2 years? Would it survive a bad phone line?

**Deliverables:** a name that passes all 3 tests + confirmed domain/handle availability + a draft logo file.

## Session 4 — Canva for Brand Assets (Posts, Stories, Reels)
**Why consistency matters:** a follower sees a post for under a second, so "There's no time to read — only to recognize."

**Moodboard dimensions (from 5 saved IG posts):** colour palette (same 2–3 colours); typography (one header font family); layout grid; filter/tone.

**Case feeds:** @breww_47 (~876 followers, 139 posts): warm coffee-toned photos, playful wordplay voice ("Pause & Pour", "the BeanBelt"), product front and centre. @thewholetruthfoods (~366K followers, ~1,200 posts): bold black-and-white palette, "big typographic claims — the words ARE the design," a repeated educational format ("one ingredient myth, busted").

**Framework — Brand Kit Lock:** "Colors, fonts, and logo lockup are fixed before a single piece of content is made." Rule: "No new colors, no new fonts mid-project. Consistency requires discipline, not talent — and it's the cheapest professionalism signal you have."
- Lock primary and secondary colours; **one heading font + one body font, "No third font, ever"**; logo in the same corner at the same size ratio on every asset.
- Breww 47 kit hex codes: Primary 1 `#2F3996`, Primary 2 `#F7F7F7`; others `#ADDDF7 #E9CCE2 #F7986A #EADDC1 #1A7A57`.

**4 formats:** Single image (fastest, one message); Carousel (step-by-step or before/after); Quote card (testimonials, bold one-liners); Story/Cover (vertical, short shelf life).

**Peer test:** "Does this look like it belongs to one consistent brand — or four different ones?" Self-test: remove the logo — would a follower still recognise the brand from colour alone? Justify colour choices against the target customer, not personal taste.

**Deliverables:** saved Canva Brand Kit + **3 posts + 1 story** (mixed formats), built only from the locked kit and ready to publish.

## Session 5 — Social Handle Setup & Bio Optimization
**Benchmark:** visitors decide in about **3 seconds** whether to follow or keep scrolling.

**Framework — The 3 Second Bio Rule:** "What you sell + who it's for + why trust you — readable in 3 seconds." "If it takes longer than 3 seconds to parse, it's too clever or too vague — rewrite it, don't defend it."
- *What you sell* — stated plainly, no jargon. *Who it's for* — the specific customer. *Why trust you* — "a number, a claim, a guarantee, or a founder story."
- Examples: @mamaearth.in (~2M): "Spreading #GoodnessInside… Toxin-Free · Cruelty-Free" (three stacked claims as proof). @trysugar (~3M): "Not here to tone it down #BoldAndFree — long-lasting makeup for every Indian skin."
- Breww 47 bio: "Fast, café-quality coffee for your work-hour rush | [X] outlets across the city | Order in one tap →"
- Pre-class bios to audit: Mamaearth, Sugar, boAt, Wakefit, Minimalist, Plum. Check whether line 1 says what they sell, whether there's a trust reason, and whether there is "one clear link, not five."

**Profile toolkit:** profile photo from the locked kit; **3–5 highlight covers** (e.g. New, Reviews, Shop, About, FAQs) in the same 1–2 colours and icon style; **link-in-bio** via Linktree or Beacons ("free tier is enough"). Instagram allows only one clickable bio link, so the tool turns it into a mini landing page (website, offer, WhatsApp, outlet locator). Task: a free Linktree with at least 2 destination links.

**Platform setup:** convert IG to a **Business account** (category tag, contact button, final bio); create a **LinkedIn company page** with the same bio structure and visuals. "Go live … for real, not a draft."

**Strategy questions:** Does your buyer decide on **impulse** (apparel drop) or **research first** (supplement)? Should the bio lead with price/offer or with trust/credibility?

**Deliverables:** live handles on 2 platforms + a bio that passes the 3-second rule + 1 highlight-cover set; submit links + an explanation of why the bio passes.

## Session 6 — Website Builders
**Why a website:** social platforms can change "overnight — or disappear"; the website is "the one asset entirely under your control."

**Builder choice:**
| Builder | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Shopify** | Selling physical products at real volume (free trial is time-boxed; start it the day you build) |
| Wix | Flexible general-purpose site |
| Notion | Fastest simple, content-first page |
| Framer | High-design, portfolio-style |
Key question: do you need a full multi-page site now, "or would one sharp landing page do the job better?"

**Framework — Above the Fold Checklist** (all 3 before any scroll): **Value Prop** (what you sell and why it matters) + **Call to Action** (clear button) + **Trust Signal** ("one real number, review, or guarantee").
- Breww 47 hero: "Café-quality coffee in minutes. / Built for your work-hour rush — not for waiting in line. / [Find Your Outlet →] / 500+ cups poured every peak-hour rush."
- Reference sites: wakefit.co (value prop + trial offer above the fold); thewholetruthfoods.com (ingredient transparency in hero); sugarcosmetics.com (bold visual identity, little text).

**Site map — 4 sections planned before building:** Home (above-fold checklist) → Product/Service → About ("why you, briefly") → Contact (one clear way to reach you).

**Checks:** use the locked brand kit; **mobile responsiveness check** (swap phones with a partner and look for anything that breaks or gets cut off). Self-test: "If you removed the text, would the layout alone still signal what you sell?"

**Deliverables:** live or draft site with a homepage, one product/service page, and a working CTA + a mobile screenshot. End of Theme 1.

## Session 7 — Email Setup + Newsletter Tool
**Why email:** with social, "an algorithm decides if your post reaches them — usually a small fraction do, and that fraction shrinks over time." Email is an owned inbox.

**Branded email:** `hello@yourbrand.com` "beats `yourbrand2024@gmail.com`" — set it up on **Google Workspace** with the S3 domain. A personal Gmail is acceptable for the exercise.

**Tool choice:**
- **Brevo** — full email marketing: automations, branded templates, list segmentation. Best if the newsletter should feel like the website.
- **Substack** — fastest start, built for writing, with built-in audience discovery. Best "if the writing itself is the product." (Also usable as a blog with SEO fields — see S15.)

**Framework — Welcome in 3** (one job per email):
1. **Thanks** — confirm signup, set expectations. (Breww 47: "Welcome to the BeanBelt — here's what's brewing": weekly brew tips + first access to new blends.)
2. **Story** — "a short, real reason this brand exists." ("Why we started brewing at 47": the founder and peak-hour insight.)
3. **Offer** — "one concrete reason to make a first purchase now." ("Your first cup is on us": first-order discount or free add-on.)

**Exercise:** write Email 1 in Brevo/Substack, send it to 5 classmates, and **check deliverability** (inbox vs spam).

**Channel-fit questions:** Does your buyer use email at your price point and age group, "or would a WhatsApp broadcast get a stronger response?" If you could send only one of the 3 emails, which one is indispensable?

**Deliverables:** branded email ID + 1 test newsletter sent + 3-email welcome sequence outline (subject line + purpose for each).

## Session 8 — IG / LinkedIn / YT Shorts Strategy
*(Extraction note: 8 of 22 slides are "VIDEO EXAMPLES · ADD HERE" placeholders with no text. The frameworks are fully captured.)*

**Mental model:** Content = **Hook** (earns the next 3 seconds) + **Format** (talking head, text-on-screen, before/after, POV), plus **Posting Time** (matched to when the audience is deciding, "not when you're free").

**Why reels fail vs succeed:** zero-view videos open with "a logo animation, a slow product pan, or 'hi guys, today I want to talk about...'". Million-view videos have "a hook in the first 3 seconds, before any branding."

**Framework — Hero, Hub, Help:**
- **Hero** — "Big, occasional, high-production": a launch or campaign moment.
- **Hub** — "Regular, personality-driven — the content people follow you for."
- **Help** — "Answers a real question — found through search, not scroll."
- Question: for low-involvement purchases, is Hero useful, or does the buyer mostly need Help?

**3 hook types:** Visual Surprise; Bold Claim ("specific enough to feel risky, not generic enough to feel safe"); Direct Question (one the viewer is "already silently asking").

**Framework — Peak Window Posting:** "Post when your audience is actually deciding." Breww 47 pushes content and offers into the 11am–4pm window rather than founder-free evenings.

**Platform differences:** **Reels** — entertainment-first, trend-aware, fast cuts. **YT Shorts** — search-discoverable, "rewards a clear payoff." **LinkedIn video** — professional context, slower pace, depends on captions.

**Deliverables:** a **1-week (7-day) content calendar** tagged Hero/Hub/Help, each slot with a post time mapped to the decision window, plus at least 2 real hooks. (This sheet is reused in S9.)

## Session 9 — 1-Month Content Calendar (Hero-Hub-Help)
**"Consistency Beats Intensity":** 15 posts in a burst followed by 3 weeks of silence means "the algorithm and the audience both forget you." **4 posts a week, every week** compounds. Pick a frequency you can hold for 4 straight weeks: "3 consistent beats 7 that stop after 10 days."

**4 content buckets (default set):**
1. **Behind the Brand** — founder voice, process, people → trust.
2. **Education** — teaches something real about the category.
3. **Customer Proof** — reviews, UGC, before/after → belief.
4. **Offer / Product** — the direct sell, "used sparingly, not every post."
"Weight your buckets deliberately, don't split evenly." Education leads for protein/skincare; entertainment for apparel.

**Theme weeks vs evergreen slots:** some weeks rally around one theme; evergreen slots stay reusable regardless of season.

**Framework — The 4x4 Grid:** 4 weeks × 4 buckets, "every slot filled before the month starts." "No empty cells — if you're stuck, that's a sign the bucket needs to change."
- Breww 47 grid: *HERO* "Behind the Brew" (founder story, roasting, '47' origin) **once a month**; *HUB* Coffee Education **weekly**; *HUB* Customer Features **weekly**; *HELP* Peak-Hour Offers timed to 11am–4pm.

**Tools:** Google Sheets or Notion free tier is enough.
**Measurement question:** "If one bucket underperforms for a full month, would you have the data to know?"

**Deliverables:** a full-month calendar with buckets and posting frequency tagged, ready to schedule. Pre-work: benchmark 2 brands' last month (buckets, frequency per bucket, which posts performed).

## Session 10 — AI in Content Creation & Influencer Discovery
**Where AI helps:** first-draft captions and hashtags; 10 idea variations in seconds; rewriting in 5 tones; shortlisting influencers from thousands of profiles. **What it doesn't replace:** brand voice and judgment, human outreach, judging true creator fit, "deciding what NOT to post."

**Prompt formula:** **Role + Brand context + Audience + Tone + Task + Constraints + What to avoid.**
- Strong example: "You are writing an Instagram caption for Breww 47, a specialty coffee outlet. Audience: working professionals, 25–35. Tone: warm, a little witty, never corporate. Write 1 caption (max 3 lines)… ending with a soft CTA. Avoid the word 'kickstart.'"

**Reusable prompt bank (4 prompts):** Caption, Hashtag, Hook, Reply (for comments). The 5 live examples were caption; **tiered hashtags** (5 broad with 1M+ posts, 5 mid-niche with 50K–500K, 5 hyperlocal; no #instagood); content ideas (8 ideas = 2 per pillar across product / people-BTS / community / offers); 5 hooks across 5 angles (curiosity, myth-busting, relatable pain, bold claim, question); and a 6-slide carousel outline (slide 1 = hook, slide 6 = CTA).

**ChatGPT setup:** (1) Custom Instructions with brand facts (category, audience, price point, 2–3 product details) and tone (3 adjectives, one thing to avoid, length); (2) a **Project** per brand with instructions + uploaded brand guidelines, best captions, and product list; (3) **Canvas** for editable outputs. "If you'll reuse it more than twice, it belongs in your prompt bank."

**Framework — Prompt to Pitch Pipeline:** AI Drafts → AI Shortlists ("by audience overlap and engagement rate, not raw follower count") → **Human Writes the Ask** (outreach stays personal).

**Influencer type by category:**
| Category | Creator type | How to search |
|---|---|---|
| F&B / cafés | Hyperlocal micro-creators (**2K–20K**) + in-city food/lifestyle reviewers | City + #cafehopping/#foodblogger; geotag |
| Beauty & personal care | Mid-tier (**20K–150K**) with tutorial/GRWM content | Discovery tools by category + ER + past collabs |
| Fashion & apparel | Style-led creators + a few macro/celeb for launch buzz | Look-book style match; check sentiment |
| D2C tech / appliances | Niche "how it works" reviewers, unboxers | YouTube + Reels; check watch-through |
| B2B / services | Founders, operators, experts | LinkedIn search by role/industry |

**4 arrangement types:** (1) **Commission/Affiliate** — % of sales via tracked code; low upfront risk, wide funnel of small creators. (2) **Awareness/Reach deal** — flat fee per post/story/reel; launches, new city. (3) **Brand Fan/Ambassador** — ongoing, gifting + small retainer. (4) **Gifting/Barter** — free product for a post; small brands, testing.

**LinkedIn vs Instagram:** IG suits visual, emotional, impulse or low-consideration buys aimed at reach. LinkedIn suits B2B and high-consideration B2C (finance, health, real estate) where the goal is credibility, thought leadership, or hiring.

**Modash workflow:** search by category, location, follower band → filter by **engagement rate** → check audience overlap (location, age, gender) → review past brand collabs → export **8–10 creators**. Benchmark: **"A 10K creator at 6% often outperforms a 100K creator at 0.8%."** Illustrative shortlist: 8.2K (92% audience overlap), 14K, 31K.

**Deliverables:** 3 GPT-generated posts + a 5-name AI-sourced influencer shortlist, each tagged with why it fits. Flag explicitly if the category may not need influencers.

## Session 11 — Influencer Collaboration Strategy & Outreach
**Why it works:** borrowed trust; cheaper storytelling ("one relatable post often out-converts a produced ad"); reusable content (becomes paid ads and site assets); hyperlocal or niche reach. **Where it doesn't:** low-trust or regulated categories (liability); no visual or narrative hook; "Brand isn't ready to fulfill a demand spike (stockouts, weak ops)"; one-off transactional purchases with no repeat value.

**Framework — Narrative → Fit → Ask → Proof:** what story the brand needs told → does the category and creator audience match it → one specific ask that proves the narrative → **one KPI decided before outreach**. "If you can't name the KPI before you reach out, you're not ready to ask yet."

**Category influencer intensity:**
| Category | Intensity |
|---|---|
| F&B, Beauty, Fashion, Lifestyle | **HIGH** (visual, habitual; trust transfers) |
| Wellness / Fitness / Personal care | **HIGH** (transformation stories) |
| D2C tech, gadgets, appliances | MEDIUM (needs demo-capable creators; slower cycle) |
| B2B / enterprise services | LOW–MED (founder/operator reach on LinkedIn) |
| Financial products, healthcare | LOW (regulatory risk; compliance-safe testimonials win) |
| Industrial / commodity B2B | LOW |

**Ask matched to narrative:**
- "Honest local alternative" → unscripted day-in-the-life or real-visit video.
- "This actually works" → before/after or side-by-side.
- "Real people pay full price" → honest review + **UGC reuse rights, no code**.
- "We're for you specifically" → co-created post in the community's format.
- Founder/origin → founder-led interview.
- Generic ask ("Can you post about our brand?") vs narrative ask ("…could you film your actual 1pm order at our outlet, no script…").

**KPI by objective (and what to ignore):**
- Awareness → reach, saves, shares (ignore follower count alone).
- Consideration → comment sentiment, click-throughs, DM inquiries (ignore likes alone).
- Conversion → **promo-code redemptions, affiliate sales, tracked orders** (ignore engagement without a code).
- Brand equity → repeat mentions, UGC rights, sentiment over time.

**Outreach message — Hook, Fit, Ask** (Breww 47 used 3 sentences total): Hook references their actual recent content → Fit explains why the audiences overlap → Ask is one low-friction step. Breww 47's KPI: "at least **3 tracked redemptions** of a unique code."

**Tiers (no ₹ rates given in the deck):**
- **Nano 1K–10K** — highest engagement, most affordable, "feels like a real recommendation."
- **Micro 10K–100K** — "the sweet spot for most small budgets."
- **Macro 100K–1M** — broad reach, lower ER, "usually needs a real budget."
- Negotiation: "Nano and micro creators often barter for product; macro usually expects a real rate card."

**Deliverables:** written strategy (narrative, fit, ask, KPI) + 3–5 real names tagged by narrative type and tier + **at least one outreach message actually sent**. If the category scores LOW on fit, write down why and where the budget should go instead.

## Session 12 — PR Hooks & Press Release Writing
**3 story angles:** **Data** (a surprising number: market share, growth, scale); **Founder story** (a personal reason the business exists); **Timing** (why it matters now).

**What journalists ignore vs cover:** "'We're excited to announce...' — excitement is not news." They cover "a specific, surprising fact their reader would want to know — with a name and a number attached."

**Framework — The So What Test:** the hook must survive "so what?" twice.
- Claim: "We launched a new flavor." → So what? "First zero-sugar cold brew made with local Indian beans." → So what? "It lets a diabetic customer order a cold brew for the first time — a segment the category has ignored."

**Press release format (1 page):** 1 **Headline** (the news in one line, no fluff) → 2 **Dateline** (city, date) → 3 **Body** (who/what/why + founder quote + data) → 4 **Boilerplate** ("About [Company]") → 5 **Contact** (name, email, phone).
- Breww 47 headline: "Delhi-based coffee outlet Breww 47 expands to 3 new office parks after 71% of daily orders land in a single 5-hour window." Dateline: "New Delhi, [Month] 2026." Boilerplate: "Breww 47 is a D2C coffee outlet brand built around speed during the workday rush."

**Direct pitch vs PR agency:** direct is free, fast, and you control the story, but needs a relationship or a strong angle. An agency costs money and ramps slowly, but brings journalist relationships. (S15 notes that PR coverage also counts as **off-page SEO**.)

**Deliverables:** 1-page press release + a 1-line media hook that passes the So What Test twice. End of Theme 2.

## Session 13 — Meta Ads Manager Basics
**Auction:** the winner is decided by three factors: **Your Bid**, **Estimated Action Rate**, and **Ad Quality** (the deck does not give a formula combining them). "A cheaper bid with a highly relevant ad can beat a bigger bid… You are not buying a slot, you are earning it."

**Framework — 1 Goal, 1 Audience, 1 Creative:** "Change three things at once and you can't tell which one worked." This maps to Ads Manager's three levels: **Campaign** = goal/objective; **Ad set** = audience, location, daily budget, schedule, placements; **Ad** = format, creative, text, headline, CTA, destination.

**Objectives:**
| Objective | Optimises for | Use when | Judge by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Reach | Nobody knows you / new area | Reach, **cost per 1,000 reached** (CPM) |
| Traffic | Link clicks | People need to see an offer page/article | Landing-page views, **cost per click** |
| Conversions (= Sales/Leads in the current UI) | A purchase/sign-up | People know you + you have data | **Cost per purchase, ROAS** |

The current menu lists Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, App promotion, Sales.

**Objective by consideration level:**
- Coffee/snacks/impulse F&B — LOW → Awareness or local Traffic.
- Fashion/beauty accessories — LOW–MED → Traffic, then Sales (retarget visitors).
- Skincare/wellness/supplements — MEDIUM → Awareness with video, then Sales ("Trust comes first").
- Appliances/gadgets/high-ticket — HIGH → Traffic to a demo, then Sales (several touches).
- B2B/subscription — HIGH → Leads.

Breww 47 chose **Traffic** (to a one-page offer with a counter code). Sales was rejected because there is no online checkout and so no purchase event. The real success measure is **offline code redemptions**.

**Audiences:** **Interest** (needs nothing; day-one default) / **Lookalike** (needs a source list, **minimum 100 people from one country**) / **Retargeting** (needs existing traffic; the pool starts at zero). The temperature ladder runs Cold (interest/location, e.g. "office workers within 1.5 km of an outlet" — start here) → Warm (e.g. people who watched 50% of a Reel) → Hot (site visitors; lookalike of redeemers). "A first campaign is almost always cold." Pick ONE audience type; don't stack.

**Budget rules:**
- Daily budgets spread spend evenly; lifetime budgets let Meta shift spend between days and enable scheduling.
- Run **at least a full week**.
- **Learning phase: about 50 optimisation results in a week** to stabilise; "small budgets may never leave it."
- **Formula: Daily budget × days = total test spend.** Check the account minimum. New accounts start with a low spend limit.

**Creative:** Single image (fast, easy to scroll past) / Video-Reel (strongest; "first 3 seconds decide everything") / Carousel ("many people never swipe past card one"). **Placements: Feed 4:5; Stories and Reels 9:16.** **Stop-Say-Do check:** STOP (interrupt in the first 3 seconds) / SAY (one message in the viewer's words) / DO (one CTA). Weak: "Fresh, premium coffee for office-goers!" Strong: opens on a tired desk at 1pm, "It's 1pm. Your inbox is winning." → pour → "Show this at the counter."

**Breww 47 mock campaign:** Goal = Traffic to an offer page with a code; Audience = interest coffee + workplace, **1.5 km around one outlet, age 22–40**; Creative = one 15-second 9:16 Reel; **Budget = ₹500/day × 7 days = ₹3,500; success = 20 code redemptions ≈ ₹175 cost per redemption.**

**Account prerequisites (in order):** (1) switch IG to professional, (2) create a FB Page, (3) link IG to the Page — these three are free; (4) Business portfolio (formerly Business Manager), (5) ad account — **choose INR and India time zone; currency and time zone can't be changed later**, (6) payment method. Running ads to a website later also needs a **Meta Pixel + verified domain**. Common errors: Page and IG not linked; not an admin; wrong currency; low spend limit; restricted account.

**Deliverables:** mock campaign screenshot showing goal, audience, creative, and daily budget + one line on why the objective fits the category's consideration level. **Draft only, never Publish.**

## Session 14 — Google Ads: Keyword Planner + Campaign Basics
**Intent beats interruption:** social ads interrupt and *create* demand; search ads respond and *capture* existing demand. "Search only catches demand that already exists. If nobody types it, no keyword can find them."

**Framework — Buyer Intent Ladder:**
1. **Informational** ("How do I…?") — learning; cheap to reach, slow to convert → teach, don't sell → guide or blog post.
2. **Comparison** ("Best…", "X vs Y") — weighing options → show your edge → comparison or proof page.
3. **Transactional** ("Buy…", "price", "near me") — ready to act; **highest intent, highest CPC** → offer + one action → offer page.
(Navigational / brand-name searches are folded into transactional.)

**Search fit by category:**
- Problem-led or commodity (insurance, laptops, **protein powder**, plumbers) — HIGH volume; "crowded, costly auctions."
- Considered purchase (**skincare**, mattresses, appliances) — MED–HIGH; comparison searches dominate.
- Local or service (cafés, salons, clinics) — LOCAL "near me"; Maps does the work.
- **Novel or discovery-led** (new snack format, new habit) — LOW; "Nobody types a name they've never heard. Create demand elsewhere first."

**Keyword Planner:** needs a Google Ads account with billing set up. Find it under Tools → Planning → Keyword Planner. Two tools: Discover new keywords / Get volume and forecasts. Workflow: 3–5 seeds → set location to India or your city → read monthly searches, competition, and cost estimate → refine and export. Reading the numbers: volume is an order of magnitude; "competition" = number of advertisers, not SEO difficulty; cost is a guide. Near-zero volume "is a finding, not a failure"; check Google Trends.

**Budget maths (illustrative):** **₹500/day ÷ ₹20 CPC = 25 clicks; 25 × 2% conversion = 0.5 results/day** (about one every two days). Bid strategy: start by optimising for **clicks** with no conversion history; move to conversions once there's data. Auction = bid + ad quality (expected CTR, ad relevance, landing-page experience).

**Campaign types:** **Search** (today's build); **Performance Max** (goal-based, assets + audience signals, Google's AI matches — "Learn the keyword logic first"); Display, Video, App, Shopping.

**Match types:** **Broad** (widest reach, least control, needs strong negatives) / **Phrase** (middle ground for small budgets) / **Exact** (tightest). First campaign: phrase + exact.
**Negative keywords:** jobs/hiring/salary; free/recipe/how to make; wrong product; cities you don't serve. Review the search terms report weekly.

**Responsive Search Ad specs:** **3–15 headlines of ≤30 characters each** (up to 3 show); **2–4 descriptions of ≤90 characters each** (up to 2 show); final URL + 2 display-path fields of ≤15 characters each. Pin only when essential (e.g. legal text). Exercise: 8 headlines = 2 with the keyword, 2 benefits, 2 differentiators, 2 CTAs; 2 descriptions (offer + why you). **Match test:** keyword, ad, and landing page must all say the same thing.

**Deliverables:** about 15 keywords tagged by intent + at least 5 negatives + one transactional RSA + one line on whether the category is search-heavy or search-light.

## Session 15 — SEO, GEO + Blog Setup
**Rented vs earned:** a social post *decays* in 1–2 days; a paid ad is *rented* ("Stop paying and it stops"); a search-friendly page *compounds* for months, but "SEO is a patient channel."

**How search works:** Crawl → Index → Rank. A page must be findable, understandable, and helpful.
**On-page** (you control): content, title, description, headings, URL, alt text, internal links. **Off-page:** backlinks, press coverage (S12 PR = off-page SEO), reviews and **Google Business Profile**, branded searches. "Buying links… breaks Google's spam policies."

**Match post to intent:** informational and comparison → blog post; transactional → offer page.
**Finding questions:** Google autocomplete, "People also ask," "Related searches," plus Google Trends, Keyword Planner, and real customer questions.

**On-page checklist:**
| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Title | about **60 characters** |
| Meta description | about **150–160 characters** |
| H1 | **One per page**, matches the title's promise |
| Subheadings | H2/H3 for each question or step |
| URL slug | short, readable, main words; set before publishing |
| Image alt text | plain description |

**Framework — 3 Pillar Blog Map:** one Pillar page (broad and thorough) + Supporting posts (each answers one question) + Internal links (up to the pillar with descriptive anchors, and back down). Breww 47 pillar: "The Complete Guide to Office Coffee" + 4 supporting posts.

**Platforms:** Substack (has SEO title, description, and slug fields; doubles as an email list); Notion (custom titles/descriptions need a paid plan; custom domain is an add-on); WordPress.com (most flexible; free plan uses a wordpress.com address).

**GEO (Generative Engine Optimization):** goal = "be quoted or cited inside an AI generated answer" (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews). Also called AEO, LLMO, GSO. Google's position: "it is still SEO"; no special markup needed; "original, first hand content with a real point of view." "Treat any GEO tactic sold as a secret with suspicion."
**Extractable Answer Test:** take a question heading, hide everything but the first 2–3 sentences, and check whether they fully answer it. **4 habits:** answer first; headings phrased as questions; claims backed by evidence (a source or "your own data"); a TL;DR of 2–3 lines on top.

**Blog launch checklist:** one question/intent; title ~60 characters; meta 150–160; one H1 + subheads; short slug; alt text on every image; links to pillar and sibling posts; a direct answer under each question heading; TL;DR; claims sourced.

**Deliverables:** one published post + the 3-pillar map sketch + one paragraph rewritten (before/after) to pass the Extractable Answer Test + which platform was used and what it wouldn't let you set.

## Session 16 — Marketplace Listings + Quick Commerce
**Why marketplaces at launch:** shoppers are already searching; payments, delivery, and returns are built in; reviews build proof; it's a cheap demand test. **Trade-offs:** fees on every sale; cheaper rivals one click away; "the platform owns the customer relationship"; ranking rules you don't control. "Use a marketplace to learn what sells. Keep building the brand you own."

**Two games:** **Self-serve marketplaces** (Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho — you list yourself; search ranking decides visibility; fees charged per sale) vs **quick commerce** (Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart — "a category manager decides whether you go live"; a supply deal into dark stores; listing fees and ad spend paid **upfront**).

**Framework — Title, Bullets, Trust:**
- **Title** = BRAND + PRODUCT + KEY DETAIL + PACK SIZE, buyer-typed words first, no shouting or offers. (Strong: "Breww 47 Specialty Coffee Beans, Medium Roast, 250 g".)
- **5 bullets**, one job each, benefit then detail: main benefit / who it's for / what's inside / proof (e.g. licence number) / reassurance (storage, returns).
- **Trust:** rating and review count; real pack photos; certificates actually held (FSSAI licence for food); brand page/store; delivery promise; public Q&A answers. Never fake reviews.
- Keyword research: marketplace search-box suggestions + 3 rival titles; put the best phrase first in the title and the next in the bullets.
- **5-image set:** (1) product on plain white, (2) pack size and contents, (3) key benefits, (4) proof (label/certificate/process), (5) in use.
- **3 price numbers:** MRP / Selling price (compare with 3 rivals) / **Net payout**. "Price from your payout backwards. Do not copy a rival's price forwards."

**Seller account requirements:**
- **Amazon India:** PAN, GSTIN, bank account, address proof, phone and email. Pays a category **referral fee + fulfilment + shipping**. A+ content needs **Brand Registry**.
- **Flipkart:** GSTIN, matching PAN, cancelled cheque, pickup address proof, signature. Pays **commission (by category and price) + fixed + collection + shipping** fees. The legal name must match across PAN, GST, and bank.
- **Meesho:** GSTIN + bank + pickup address. Marketed as **zero commission**; check shipping and ad terms. Price-led platform.
- "**GST registration is needed from the first marketplace sale**, even below the usual turnover limit."

**Net payout waterfall (illustrative, not a real rate card):**
| Line | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Selling price | 500 |
| Commission 10% | −50 |
| Fixed fee | −20 |
| Shipping fee | −60 |
| **GST 18% on fees** | −23.4 |
| **TCS 1%** | −5 |
| **Net payout** | **341.6** |
| COGS | −150 |
| Left before ads, returns, packaging | **191.6 (₹38 of every ₹100)** |
TCS is claimable later, so it is a timing effect, not a cost.

**Quick commerce onboarding:** Apply → category manager approval → agree terms (margin, listing fee, ad commitment) → supply stock via POs to the warehouse and dark stores. **Onboarding 20–60 days**; approval is not guaranteed. Slow stock can come back at your cost.
- Requirements: GSTIN (matching legal name), **FSSAI** for food and drink, **GS1 barcode on every SKU**, a registered business, complete labels. Informal demand bar (one agency's example): **200+ orders/month and 4.2+ rating elsewhere**. Rejection reasons: document mismatches, missing barcodes, label gaps, near-identical products, prices that can't absorb commission.

**Reported quick-commerce costs (2026 press and agency reports, not official):**
- **Blinkit:** **₹25,000 per SKU per state** (credited to an ad wallet with 12-month expiry); **monthly marketing ₹2–3 lakh**; **commission 8–20%** by category.
- **Zepto:** bundled entry package **₹5–6 lakh** (banners, in-feed placement, brand store); onboarding 4–8 weeks.
- **Swiggy Instamart:** quarterly brand pack **₹8–10 lakh**; suits already-known brands.
- **Commission + ad spend ≈ 30–35% of selling price**, before COGS.
- Worked example: 5 SKUs × 3 states on Blinkit = **₹3,75,000 listing fee** + ₹2–3 lakh/month ads ⇒ **₹6–9 lakh in the first quarter**, before commission, stock, and packaging.

**Framework — The 65% Gross Margin Gate:**
**Gross margin = (Selling price − COGS) ÷ Selling price.** E.g. (500−150)/500 = 70%. Economics turn "structurally negative below about 60%"; the real bar is 65–70%; the **course gate is 65%**. Bands: <60% / 60–65% / ≥65%. "Miss the gate and quick commerce is not a channel decision yet. It is a pricing or cost problem."
| GM | COGS | Commission+ads (33%) | Left per unit | % of price | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 70% | ₹150 | ₹165 | ₹185 | 37% | Clears |
| 65% | ₹175 | ₹165 | ₹160 | 32% | Clears, thin |
| 40% | ₹300 | ₹165 | ₹35 | 7% | Fails |
| 30% | ₹350 | ₹165 | −₹15 | −3% | Loses money |
What's left must still cover packaging, dark-store logistics, discounts, returns, and working capital. Because ad spend is a minimum commitment, low sales push the real share well above 33%.

**The ROAS Trap:** dashboards may count revenue at **MRP**. Example: 6 orders × MRP ₹500 = ₹3,000 / ₹1,000 spend = **3.0x shown**, but 6 × ₹340 actually paid = ₹2,040 → **2.04x real** (about 47% inflated). Fix: compute ROAS on **net transaction value** (what the customer paid). "**Small brands are reported at 1.2x to 1.5x real ROAS.**"
**Cautionary case (reported, unverified):** ₹6 lakh spent listing on 3 platforms → ₹1.2 lakh *revenue* in 3 months; another seller's sales didn't reach 10% of spend. Lessons: "Fix margin before reach. Count revenue at the price paid. **Set a stop rule before you pay a listing fee.**"

**Deliverables:** mock listing screenshot (title, 5 bullets, price + MRP, one trust signal) + gross-margin figure with working + a one-line quick-commerce verdict (clears / close / fails, plus the first fix).

## Session 17 — ATL on a Budget
*(Extraction note: about 3 slides are image-only — the ATL vs BTL discussion opener and ad examples from The Economist, KitKat, Nescafé, and Nike. The text is otherwise complete.)*

**ATL vs BTL:**
- **ATL (above the line)** = mass media (TV, radio, newspapers, billboards). Best at making many people know your name. "Big cheques, hard to count."
- **BTL (below the line)** = targeted (events, sampling, flyers, offers, DMs). Best at getting a chosen person to act. "Smaller cheques, easier to count."
- Why ATL matters for small brands: "People buy what they recognise. A familiar name makes every other channel work better." At small scale, that means "a poster where your buyer walks every day, not a TV ad." **In this course, ATL = any offline marketing you can afford.**

**3 decisions, in order:** (1) Single Minded Proposition → (2) Channel and format (+ how you'll count) → (3) Budget.

**Framework — Single Minded Proposition (SMP):** one promise, one audience, readable in 3 seconds. "Too much: Premium, fresh, affordable, sustainable coffee for everyone" vs "**Better coffee. At your desk.**" Template: "[Brand] helps [who] [one benefit]"; headline of **5 words or fewer**.

**Low-cost offline channels + how to count them:**
- **Posters** → QR code (**one code per poster** to compare locations).
- **Local tie-ups** → a code word per partner.
- **Events** → sign-up sheet or WhatsApp link.
- Permissions: walls, society boards, and hoardings need approval; "In Gurugram, outdoor ads need the municipal corporation's permission." For online-only brands: community group posts, one nearby creator, or a small online event.
- "If you cannot count it, you cannot learn from it."

**Framework — 60/30/10 Budget Split:** **60% Safe bet / 30% Test / 10% Wild card.** On ₹10,000: ₹6,000 / ₹3,000 / ₹1,000. "A rule of thumb, not an industry standard."

**Breww 47 real posters (₹1,000 each):**
1. *Feedback poster* at the outlet: "Don't just sip the next wave. Build it." / "Spend 2 minutes with us while we prepare your coffee." Counted via QR.
2. *SmartWorks price poster* (local tie-up with the coworking chain): "If your coffee looks expensive, that's style! If it starts at **₹79**, that's smart." / "Enjoy our exclusive pricing at SmartWorks outlets." Counted via QR (scan, like, share, join #thenextwave).

**ATL Planning Sheet (handout):** Name / Brand / Budget ₹ → (1) One promise, (2) Channel and place, (3) Budget split 60/30/10 in ₹, (4) How we'll count. Check: one promise, one audience, split adds up, response is countable.

**Deliverables:** an ATL activation idea + a 60/30/10 budget split on the ATL Planning Sheet.

## Session 18 — Media Plan Creation & Cost Estimation
*(Extraction note: the "Who messaged you?" discussion slide is image-only. Everything else is captured.)*

**Framework — The 3 Channel Funnel:** **Paid** (buy attention: Meta/Google ads; fast, but you pay per view) → **Organic** (earn attention: posts, content, word of mouth, posters; slower, builds trust, no media bill) → **Conversational** (keep the conversation: WhatsApp, email, SMS; "turns interest into orders and orders into repeat"). A creator counts as paid if you pay them and organic if they post on their own. Exercise: list 6+ channels, sort each into one column, circle the expected top seller.

**Good vs bad examples:**
- Paid good: "Cold brew for your 11am slump. Delivered to your office desk. First pack 20% off. Order today." (one audience, one offer, one action)
- Organic good: "3 ways to make café coffee at your desk. Save this for Monday."
- WhatsApp good: "Hi Riya, thanks for your first order… ships tomorrow. Reply 1 to change your address. Reply STOP to opt out." Bad: "BIG SALE!!! 50% OFF… forward this to 10 friends" (shouting, two asks, no consent).

**WhatsApp Business setup:** either use your existing number (migrate to the Business app; it can't run on both apps) or a new number (2nd SIM/eSIM/landline). **5 steps:** business profile (name, description, hours, address) → **Catalog** → greeting message → quick replies + labels (New/Paid/Repeat) → away message. The app is free. **Broadcast lists reach up to 256 people who have saved your number.** Paid API tools (**Interakt, AiSensy**) are optional and need a Meta business portfolio + verification.

**WhatsApp funnel (one action each, always with opt-out):** First message (who, why, one action) → Follow-up nudge → After-purchase (thanks, one ask, a way to stop).

**Metrics (the course's core formulas):**
- **ROAS = Revenue from ads ÷ Ad spend** (one channel or campaign: "Did this ad pay for itself?")
- **MER = Total revenue ÷ Total marketing spend** (whole business: "Is all our marketing paying off?") MER counts sales the ads don't claim, e.g. WhatsApp repeat orders.
- **Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ Gross margin.** At 65% → **1.54**. "Below that, each sale loses money before other costs." With no margin yet, use **60% → 1.67**.

**Worked example (1 month):** Revenue ₹4,00,000; Meta+Google spend ₹80,000; revenue credited to ads ₹2,00,000; other marketing (creators, WhatsApp tool, posters) ₹70,000; total marketing ₹1,50,000. **ROAS = 2.5; MER = 2.67; BE ROAS = 1.54 → clears.**
Class exercise: revenue ₹6L, ad spend ₹1.5L, ad-credited revenue ₹3L, other marketing ₹50K, GM 60%. *(My computation: ROAS = 2.0; MER = 6L ÷ 2L = 3.0; BE ROAS = 1.67 → ads clear.)*

**Budget split method:** (1) fix one monthly total in ₹; (2) give each channel a job and a share (paid → new buyers, organic → trust, WhatsApp → repeat and follow-ups); (3) check against your numbers ("If a channel's ROAS beats break even, move more money to it. If not, cut it."). "Most money on what you can already measure, a small share to test the rest."

**Breww 47 Media Plan Sheet (₹1,00,000/month):**
| Channel | Job | Activity | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid | New office workers | Meta ads near campuses | **60% — ₹60,000** |
| Organic | Build trust | IG posts + campus posters | **25% — ₹25,000** |
| WhatsApp | Bring them back | Reminder before 11am–4pm, feedback after | **15% — ₹15,000** |
**Target ROAS (paid) 2.5** vs break-even 1.54; **target MER 3.0**.

**Final build steps:** assign ₹ budgets per funnel step summing to one total → **Revenue guess = orders × order value** per step (this is AOV logic, though the deck doesn't use the term) → compute ROAS and MER → **pass if paid ROAS > break-even and MER ≥ target.**

**Deliverables:** a GTM media plan on the Media Plan Sheet with at least one WhatsApp/CRM channel + stated ROAS and MER targets.

## D. Recommended order for launching a D2C brand (per the course sequence)
1. **Validate the problem** (S1): a named customer who has paid to solve it or complained about it; Search Test + 3-Person Test; write the PSC sentence; set 3 measurable goals; find the smallest version testable in 2 weeks.
2. **Lean Canvas with real numbers** (S2): price point and COGS → *compute gross margin now* (it drives S16 and S18).
3. **Name, domain, handle, logo** (S3): Say/Spell/Search; buy .com or .in; secure the IG handle; 1–2 colour logo.
4. **Lock the brand kit** (S4): colours, 2 fonts, logo placement; produce first posts and stories.
5. **Set up social profiles** (S5): IG Business + LinkedIn page; 3-second bio; highlights; Linktree.
6. **Build the website** (S6): Shopify for physical products; above-the-fold checklist; mobile check.
7. **Owned audience via email** (S7): hello@domain on Google Workspace; Brevo/Substack; Welcome-in-3. Consider WhatsApp if the buyer prefers it.
