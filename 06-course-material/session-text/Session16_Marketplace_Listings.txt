KICKOFF  ·  0–4 MIN
Why Marketplaces Are Still a Launch Channel
WHY LIST THERE
•   Shoppers are already there, searching for products
•   Payments, delivery and returns tooling come built in
•   Reviews build proof you can point to later
•   A cheap way to test whether anyone wants the product
THE TRADE OFF
•   Fees come off every single sale
•   You sit next to cheaper rivals, one click away
•   The platform owns the customer relationship
•   Your ranking depends on rules you do not control
Use a marketplace to learn what sells. Keep building the brand you own.

KICKOFF  ·  4–8 MIN
Same Word, Two Different Games
SELF SERVE MARKETPLACE
Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho
•   You register and list your own products
•   Search ranking decides who gets seen
•   Fees are charged when you make a sale
QUICK COMMERCE
Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart
•   A category manager decides whether you go live
•   It is a supply deal: you stock their dark stores
•   Listing fees and ad spend are paid upfront
Today we learn to win the first game, and to check honestly whether you can afford the second.

KEYWORD RESEARCH  ·  16–18 MIN
Keywords: Use the Marketplace Search Box
The same idea as Session 15, applied to the platform where your listing lives.
1
Type your product
Start typing in the search box on Amazon, 
Flipkart or Meesho. The suggestions show 
what shoppers on that platform type.
2
Read 3 rival titles
Open the top three listings. Note the words 
that repeat in their titles and bullets.
3
Place the best two
Put the strongest phrase at the start of the 
title and the next in the bullets. No stuffing.
Write for the buyer first. A title that reads like a keyword pile gets skipped.

QUICK COMMERCE  ·  32–36 MIN
Marketplace vs Quick Commerce, Side by Side
SELF SERVE MARKETPLACE QUICK COMMERCE
Who lets you in Account approval, then you list yourself A category manager approves each SKU
How you get seen Search ranking, reviews and ads Placement you buy with ad credits and banners
Cost to start Registration is usually free; fees come per sale A listing fee per SKU and a monthly ad commitment
Who holds stock You, or the platform's fulfilment warehouse Dark stores, supplied on purchase orders
If it does not sell Reprice or pause the listing Reports say slow stock can come back at your cost
Terms differ by platform, brand and city. Always confirm with the platform.

QUICK COMMERCE  ·  36–41 MIN
What Quick Commerce Platforms Look For
LEGAL BASICS
•   GSTIN, with the legal name matching your papers
•   FSSAI licence for food and drink, not lapsed
•   GS1 barcode on every SKU
•   A registered business, not a private individual
•   Labels with every mandatory field filled in
A DEMAND SIGNAL
No platform publishes its bar. The usual advice is proof that you already 
sell, such as 200+ orders a month and a 4.2+ rating elsewhere.
One agency's example, not a platform rule
COMMON REASONS FOR REJECTION
Mismatched documents, missing barcodes, label gaps, near identical 
products, prices that cannot absorb the commission.
Today nobody applies. These are things to build towards.

FRAMEWORK  ·  45–48 MIN
The 65% Gross Margin Gate
GROSS MARGIN
(Selling price − Cost of goods) ÷ Selling price
Example: sells at ₹500, costs ₹150 to make or buy. (500 − 150) ÷ 500 = 70%
BELOW 60% 60 to 65 65% AND ABOVE
40% 60% 65% 100%
Reports say the economics turn structurally negative below about 60%. Sources put the real bar between 65% and 70%. We use 65% as the course gate.
Miss the gate and quick commerce is not a channel decision yet. It is a pricing or cost problem to fix first.

WORKED EXAMPLE  ·  51–55 MIN
Why the Gate Sits So High
Illustrative example
One ₹500 product. Commission plus ads taken as 33% of the price (₹165), inside the reported 30% to 35%.
GROSS MARGIN COST OF GOODS COMMISSION + ADS LEFT PER UNIT OF THE PRICE VERDICT
70% ₹150 ₹165 ₹185 37% Clears the gate
65% ₹175 ₹165 ₹160 32% Clears, thin cushion
40% ₹300 ₹165 ₹35 7% Fails, fix this first
30% ₹350 ₹165 −₹15 −3% Fails, loses money
What is left must still pay for packaging, dark store logistics, discounts, returned stock and cash tied up. Ad spend is a minimum commitment, so low sales 
push the real share far above 33%.

Run Your Own Margin Check
1 Write down your selling price and your cost of goods per unit, using your Session 2 Lean Canvas numbers.
2 Work out your gross margin, (price − cost) ÷ price, and place it on the gate: below 60%, 60 to 65%, or 65% and above.
3 Write your one line verdict: clears, close, or fails, plus the first thing you would change.
No platform is contacted. This is your own number against a reported threshold.

THE ROAS TRAP  ·  59–63 MIN
Dashboard ROAS Can Be Counted on MRP
Illustrative example
ROAS = revenue from ads ÷ ad spend. A dashboard shows it for you, but which revenue does it count?
WHAT THE DASHBOARD SHOWS
3.0x
6 orders × MRP ₹500 = ₹3,000
÷ ad spend ₹1,000
Counts the printed price, not what customers paid.
WHAT YOU ACTUALLY EARNED
2.04x
6 orders × paid ₹340 = ₹2,040
÷ ad spend ₹1,000
About 47% higher on the dashboard, from the price gap alone.
THE FIX
Recalculate ROAS yourself on net transaction value, the amount the customer actually paid. Small brands are reported at 1.2x to 1.5x real 
ROAS.
