WHAT IS SEO?  ·  0–5 MIN
SEO: Earning a Place in Search Results
SEARCH ENGINE OPTIMIZATION
Making your pages easy for search engines to find, 
understand and rank, so the right people reach you 
without you paying for each click.
Search engine: Google, Bing and others. Most people in India start with Google.
A SEARCH RESULTS PAGE
Ad  A paid result. You pay per click.
Organic result  An earned result. Nobody pays for the click.
Organic result  Ranked by how helpful and relevant it looks.
Organic result  This is the slot SEO is about.
Session 14 was the paid slot at the top. Today is the earned slots below it.

WHY SEO  ·  5–9 MIN
Rented Attention vs Earned Attention
DECAYS
Social post
Strong for a day or two, then it sinks in 
the feed.
RENTED
Paid ad
Works as long as the budget runs. Stop 
paying and it stops.
COMPOUNDS
Search friendly page
Can keep bringing the right people for 
months, without a daily spend.

HOW SEO WORKS  ·  9–13 MIN
How a Search Engine Works in Three Steps
1
Crawl
Automated programs follow links across the 
web and discover pages.
→
2
Index
The engine reads each page, works out what 
it is about, and stores it.
→
3
Rank
When someone searches, it picks the most 
helpful pages from what it stored.
SO YOUR PAGE MUST BE
Findable (public and linked to)   ·   Understandable (clear words and headings)   ·   Helpful (worth ranking)

HOW SEO WORKS  ·  13–16 MIN
Two Sides of SEO: On Page and Off Page
ON PAGE
Everything you control on your own site
•   Content that fully answers the question
•   Title, description and headings
•   Link address and image alt text
•   Links between your own pages
OFF PAGE
Signals that come from outside your site
•   Links from other websites to yours
•   Press coverage and mentions
•   Reviews and local business profiles
•   People searching for your brand by name
Our order today: on page first, then off page and the blog, then AI answers, then you build.

ON PAGE SEO  ·  19–23 MIN
Where to Find What People Ask
Three places inside Google Search itself. Try them on your own topic as we go.
The search box
Click the Google search box and start typing 
your topic. The suggestions that drop down 
are searches people really make.
People also ask
On the results page, look for the box titled 
People also ask. Each question opens to 
show an answer.
Related searches
Scroll to the bottom of the results page. 
The Related searches list shows what 
people look for next.
More sources: Google Trends, Keyword Planner from Session 14, and the questions your own customers ask you.

ON PAGE SEO  ·  23–28 MIN
The On Page Checklist
Title Says what the post is about. Keep it to roughly 60 characters. The page title shown in results
Meta description A short summary that earns the click. Roughly 150 to 160 characters. The grey text under the title
H1 heading One per page. It matches the topic and the promise of the title. The big heading at the top
Subheadings Use H2 and H3 for each question or step, in order. The outline of the page
Link address Short, readable, with the main words. Set it before you publish. The end of the web address
Image alt text A plain description of the image for readers who cannot see it. Hidden text on the image

GEO  ·  46–49 MIN
SEO vs GEO: A Click or a Citation
SEO
Search Engine Optimization
Goal: rank in a list of links so the person clicks through to your 
page.
GEO
Generative Engine Optimization
Goal: be quoted or cited inside an AI generated answer, such as 
ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews.
The field also goes by AEO, LLMO and GSO. The names are young and unsettled, so check what a source means before trusting it.
SCALING A D2C BUSINESS  ·  SESSION 15 GEO
--- page 16 ---
GEO  ·  49–52 MIN
What Google Actually Says About AI Search
It is still SEO
Google says optimizing for its AI search 
features is optimizing for the search 
experience, and so still SEO. Good SEO is the 
base.
No special tricks needed
No special markup, AI only files or tiny 
chopped up content are required. Write for 
people, with clear headings.
Be worth citing
Original, first hand content with a real point 
of view beats a summary of what others 
already said.
Treat any GEO tactic sold as a secret with suspicion. Clear writing helps people and machines alike, so that is what we practise.

GEO  ·  52–55 MIN
The Extractable Answer Test
1
Pick a heading
Choose a section whose heading is a 
question people ask.
2
Cover the rest
Hide everything except the first 2 to 3 
sentences under it.
3
Does it stand alone?
If those sentences fully answer the 
heading, the section passes.
WHY IT WORKS AS A TEST
A reader who skims, and a machine that lifts one passage, both meet your section out of context. If the opening lines stand alone, you served 
both.

GEO  ·  55–58 MIN
Same Section, Before and After
Illustrative copy
Heading: What is an office coffee subscription?
FAILS THE TEST
Coffee has always been part of office life, and in recent years more and more companies have started wondering how best to keep their teams 
caffeinated. There are many options out there...
PASSES THE TEST
An office coffee subscription is a monthly plan where a supplier provides and serves coffee for a workplace, usually billed per seat or per cup. 
It replaces buying beans and maintaining a machine yourself.
The first version makes a reader and an AI engine both scroll to find the answer. The second gives it away at once, then adds detail below.

WRITING FOR EXTRACTION  ·  58–60 MIN
Four Habits That Make a Section Clear
Answer first
Open each section with the direct answer, then explain.
Headings that are questions
Phrase subheadings the way a customer would ask them.
Claims with evidence
Name a source, or say it is your own data. No unexplained 
numbers.
A short summary on top
A TL;DR of two or three lines helps skimmers and tools.
Write for people first. These habits are just good writing.
