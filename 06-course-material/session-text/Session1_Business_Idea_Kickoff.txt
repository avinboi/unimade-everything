Why Most Startup Ideas Fail Before Launch
Solution-first thinking
Founders fall in love with the solution 
before validating the problem exists.
"I think people will want 
this"
A hunch is not evidence. It's a starting 
point for a test, not a launch decision.
Zero demand, not bad 
execution
Most ideas die because nobody was 
hurting enough to pay — not because 
the product was badly built.

10 – 25 MIN (CONT.)
Spotting Real Problems vs Assumed Problems
REAL PROBLEM
Something you or someone you know has personally 
paid to solve, or actively complained about.
ASSUMED PROBLEM
Something that sounds logical but no one has ever 
actually paid to fix.
THE TEST: can you name one specific person who has this problem today?

FRAMEWORK  ·  25–35 MIN
The Problem-Solution-Customer Sentence
For [target customer] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [alternative], 
[differentiation].
THE SHORTER VERSION
"We help X do Y by doing Z."  — Steve Blank
Every word must be specific. No "everyone." No "a solution for X."

--- page 8 ---
WORKED EXAMPLE  ·  A REAL BUSINESS
The Framework in Action: Breww 47
For office-goers who want good coffee without the café wait, Breww 47 is a quick-service coffee 
outlet that delivers café-quality coffee in minutes. Unlike a sit-down café, Breww 47 is built for speed 
at peak hours — not ambience.
THE SHORTER VERSION
"We help office-goers get great coffee fast by running quick-service outlets built for peak-hour speed."
71–74% of Breww 47's daily orders land between 11am and 4pm — this sentence isn't a guess, it's built on that same real order data.
