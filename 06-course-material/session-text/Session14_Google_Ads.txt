KICKOFF  ·  5–10 MIN
Intent Beats Interruption
Session 13 interrupted people who were not looking. Search is the opposite.
SOCIAL ADS INTERRUPT
•   Someone is scrolling and you step in front of them
•   You create demand the person did not know they had
•   The creative has to earn attention in three seconds
•   You choose the audience; the person never asked
SEARCH ADS RESPOND
•   Someone types exactly what they want and you answer
•   You capture demand that already exists
•   The words they typed decide whether you show up
•   The person chose the topic; you choose whether to respond
The catch: search only catches demand that already exists. If nobody types it, no keyword can find them.

Does Your Category Actually Get Searched?
Problem led or commodity Insurance, laptops, protein powder, plumbers HIGH People already search. Expect crowded, costly auctions.
Considered purchase Skincare, mattresses, appliances MED–HIGH Comparison searches dominate. Proof matters.
Local or service Cafés, salons, clinics LOCAL 'Near me' searches. Maps and location do the work.

KEYWORD PLANNER  ·  35–39 MIN
Reading the Numbers Without Fooling Yourself
Volume is an estimate
It may show as a range. Treat it as order of 
magnitude, not an exact count.
Competition means 
advertisers
It shows how many advertisers bid on the 
term. It is not how hard it is to rank on 
Google.
Cost is a guide
The estimated cost to show an ad reflects 
what advertisers pay. Your real cost per click 
can differ.
NEAR ZERO VOLUME?
Say it openly. For a discovery led category that is a finding, not a failure. Check Google Trends for relative interest and write down what that 
means for your budget.

BUDGET & BIDDING  ·  39–43 MIN
Budget and Bidding Basics
Daily budget
The average you are willing to spend per day. 
Google may spend a bit more or less on a given 
day.
Cost per click
What you pay when someone clicks. Often less 
than your maximum bid.
Bid strategy
With no conversion history, start by optimizing 
for clicks. Move to conversions once you have 
data.
Illustrative numbers
₹500 a day ÷ ₹20 per click = 25 clicks
25 clicks × 2% who convert = 0.5 results a day, about one every two days.
Read the real cost estimate off Keyword Planner, then redo the sum.
WHO WINS THE AUCTION?
Your bid plus ad quality: expected click rate, ad relevance 
and landing page experience. Same idea as Meta: relevance 
can beat a bigger bid.

KEYWORD LIST  ·  45–49 MIN
Match Types: How Closely a Search Must Fit
Example keyword: office coffee subscription
BROAD Your ad can show for searches related to the keyword, even without its words. Widest reach. Least control. Needs strong negatives.
PHRASE Your ad can show for searches that include the meaning of the keyword. A good middle ground for most small budgets.
EXACT Your ad can show for searches with the same meaning as the keyword. Tightest control. Lowest reach.
For a first campaign: start with phrase and exact. Use broad only with a tight negatives list and room to spend.

KEYWORD LIST  ·  49–53 MIN
Negative Keywords: Searches You Refuse to Pay For
A negative keyword stops your ad from showing when a search contains that word. It protects budget from clicks that were never 
going to buy.
Jobs and careers
jobs, hiring, salary
Free and DIY
free, recipe, how to make
Wrong product
instant coffee, machine repair
Wrong place
cities you do not serve
Once a campaign runs, review the search terms report each week and turn wasteful queries into new negatives.

SEARCH AD  ·  65–69 MIN
Anatomy of a Responsive Search Ad
You supply the pieces. Google tests combinations and learns which ones work.
3 to 15
Headlines
Each up to 30 characters. Up to 3 can show 
together.
2 to 4
Descriptions
Each up to 90 characters. Up to 2 can show 
together.
1 page
Final URL and path
Where the click lands, plus a short display 
path of two parts up to 15 characters each.
Pin a headline to a position only if it must always appear, such as legal text. Pinning limits what Google can test.

72–76 MIN  ·  LIVE BUILD, PART 2 OF 3
Write the Headlines for Your Transactional Ad
1 Pick your best transactional keyword and write 8 headlines of 30 characters or fewer.
2 Make sure 2 of them include your keyword, 2 state a benefit, 2 state what is different, and 2 are a call to action.
3 Count the characters in each one. Cut until every headline fits.
Headlines get mixed in any order, so each one must make sense alone.

76–80 MIN  ·  LIVE BUILD, PART 3 OF 3
Write the Descriptions and Run the Match Test
1 Write 2 descriptions of 90 characters or fewer: one on the offer, one on why you.
2 Choose the page the click will land on and check it delivers exactly what the ad promised.
3 Screenshot or save your keyword list and your ad together.
Match test: keyword, ad and landing page must all say the same thing.
