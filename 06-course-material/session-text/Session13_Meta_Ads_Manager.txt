KICKOFF  ·  5–10 MIN
How the Meta Ads Auction Actually Works
Every time someone opens Instagram or Facebook, a tiny instant auction decides which ads they see.
Your Bid
What you're willing to pay for the result 
you asked for.
Estimated Action Rate
How likely this person is to take the action 
you're optimizing for.
Ad Quality
How relevant and engaging the ad is likely 
to be for this specific person.
A cheaper bid with a highly relevant ad can beat a bigger bid with a generic one. You are not buying a slot, you are earning it.

CAMPAIGN OBJECTIVES  ·  16–20 MIN
The Objective Tells Meta What to Optimize For
The most common beginner mistake is picking an objective that doesn't match the real goal.
OBJECTIVE OPTIMIZES FOR USE IT WHEN JUDGE IT BY
Awareness Reach: the most people for your money Nobody knows you yet, or you're launching in a new area Reach, cost per 1,000 people reached
Traffic Link clicks to a place you choose You need people to see an offer page, menu or article Landing page views, cost per click
Conversions A specific action, like a purchase or sign-up People already know you and you have data to learn from Cost per purchase, return on ad spend
In today's Ads Manager the menu is longer (Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, App promotion, Sales). Conversions maps to Sales and Leads.

CAMPAIGN OBJECTIVES  ·  20–23 MIN
Match the Objective to How Long Buyers Think
Coffee, snacks, impulse F&B LOW Awareness or Traffic, local Short decision. Reach people near you and make trying it easy.
Fashion, beauty accessories LOW–MED Traffic, then Sales Browsing is part of buying. Retarget visitors once you have some.
Skincare, wellness, supplements MEDIUM Awareness with video, then Sales Trust comes first. Show proof before asking for a purchase.
Appliances, gadgets, high-ticket HIGH Traffic to a demo, then Sales People compare and wait. Plan for several touches before a sale.
B2B or subscription services HIGH Leads The goal is a conversation, not a checkout.
Where do you land? Say your category and your starting objective out loud.

WORKED EXAMPLE  ·  23–25 MIN
Breww 47: Choosing the Goal
Illustrative example
Context: orders are placed at the counter, and 71–74% of them land between 11am and 4pm.
Conversions (Sales) REJECTED There's no online checkout. The purchase happens at an outlet, so Meta has no purchase event to learn from.
Awareness POSSIBLE Fine for a new campus launch, but reach alone can't tell us if anyone walked in.
Traffic CHOSEN Send people to a one-page offer with a code to show at the counter. A click is measurable and so is the redemption.

AUDIENCE TARGETING  ·  25–31 MIN
Three Ways to Choose Who Sees Your Ad
Interest
People who follow related pages, topics and 
behaviors.
NEEDS
Nothing but a clear idea of your buyer.
BEST WHEN
Day one. This is the default for a brand with no 
data.
Lookalike
People who resemble your existing 
customers.
NEEDS
A source list of real customers or engaged 
people. Meta needs a minimum of 100 from 
one country.
BEST WHEN
Once you have a customer list worth copying.
Retargeting
People who already visited your site or 
engaged with you.
NEEDS
Traffic or engagement to retarget. The pool 
starts at zero.
BEST WHEN
After you've been visible for a while.
A first campaign picks ONE of these. Most should start narrow, not stacked.

AUDIENCE TARGETING  ·  31–35 MIN
Which Audience Fits Which Stage?
Cold Never heard of you Interest or location Office workers within 1.5 km of an outlet YES, start here
Warm Watched, liked or followed Retargeting on engagement People who watched 50% of last week's Reel Later
Hot Visited or bought Retargeting or Lookalike Offer-page visitors; lookalike of past redeemers Later
A brand with no data has no warm or hot audience. That's why a first campaign is almost always cold.

AUDIENCE TARGETING  ·  35–40 MIN
Stating a Budget Without Guessing
Your deliverable needs a stated daily budget. Here is how to choose one on purpose.
Daily vs lifetime
Daily spreads spend evenly. Lifetime lets Meta shift spend to better days and enables ad 
scheduling.
Duration
Run at least a full week so one odd day doesn't decide the result.
The learning phase
Meta needs roughly 50 optimization results in a week to stabilize delivery. Small budgets may 
never leave it.
A SIMPLE WAY TO SET IT
Daily budget × days = total test spend
Check the account's minimum daily budget in Ads Manager 
before you commit.

BEFORE THE DEMO  ·  50–54 MIN
What You Need Before You Can Run a Single Ad
Five pieces sit behind every Meta ad. Miss one and Ads Manager will stop you.
1 Personal Facebook profile Your login to everything Meta. It stays private and is never shown on your ads. FREE
2 Facebook Page The public identity of your brand. Your ads run as this Page. FREE
3 Instagram professional account Business or Creator type, linked to the Page. Lets ads run under your Instagram handle. FREE
4 Business portfolio The container that holds your Page, Instagram account and ad account. Formerly called Business Manager. FREE
5 Ad account and payment method Where spend is billed. Currency and time zone cannot be changed later. NEEDS PAYMENT
Running ads to a website later also needs a Meta Pixel and a verified domain. We skip both today.

BEFORE THE DEMO  ·  58–60 MIN
Where Registration Usually Goes Wrong
Page and Instagram are not linked Reconnect them in the Page settings or in the business portfolio settings, then refresh Ads Manager.
You are not an admin Ask whoever created the Page or account to grant you full control. Partial access blocks ad creation.
Wrong currency or time zone It is permanent for that ad account. Create a new ad account with the right settings.
Very low starting spend limit New accounts usually start with a limit that grows with a clean payment history. Plan a smaller first test.
Account or ad restricted Read the Advertising Standards, fix the flagged issue, and request a review. Use your real profile with two factor sign in.
