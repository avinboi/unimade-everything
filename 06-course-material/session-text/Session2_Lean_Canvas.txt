0 – 10 MIN
Why a 40-Page Plan Fails, a 1-Page Canvas Doesn't
A plan documents 
assumptions
As if they were already facts — nothing 
on the page has been tested against 
reality yet.
A canvas exposes 
assumptions
Every box is a guess you can go test this 
week, not a paragraph you defend in a 
meeting.
Speed to test beats 
completeness
A canvas you can rewrite in 20 minutes 
beats a plan that took 3 weeks to format.
SCALING A D2C BUSINESS  ·  SESSION 2 Kickoff
--- page 5 ---
10 – 45 MIN
Box Deep Dive: Problem → Solution
Problem
List the top 3 problems your customer has, ranked by how much 
they hurt.
Existing Alternatives
What does your customer currently do instead of buying from 
you? Name the real competitor.
Solution
Your top 3 features, mapped directly to the top 3 problems above 
— no extras yet.
Unique Value Proposition
One sentence: why you're different and worth a second look, not a 
generic promise.

--- page 6 ---
10 – 45 MIN (CONT.)
Box Deep Dive: Channels → Cost Structure
Channels
How will customers actually discover and buy from you — be 
specific, not 'social media'.
Customer Segments
Your specific early-adopter segment, not 'everyone who needs X'.
Revenue Streams
Your pricing model and expected price point — a real number, not 
a placeholder.
Cost Structure
Your biggest cost line items — a real estimate, even if rough.
SCALING A D2C BUSINESS  ·  SESSION 2 Box Deep Dive
--- page 7 ---
FRAMEWORK
The 9 Box Reality Check
Every box must carry a real number —not a placeholder like “TBD” or “a lot.”
PLACEHOLDER
“We'll get a lot of customers.”
REAL NUMBER
“450 orders/month from 3 nearby office parks.”
If a box has no real number, that's this week's homework — not something to guess.

--- page 8 ---
WORKED EXAMPLE  ·  A REAL BUSINESS
The Lean Canvas in Action: Breww 47
Customer Segments
Office-goers & urban professionals near outlets
Problem
Long café queues; bad coffee at home; no fast option in 
the work-hour rush
Existing Alternatives
Sit-down cafés (slow), instant coffee, vending machines
Unique Value Prop.
Café-quality coffee in minutes — built for speed, not 
ambience
Solution
Quick-service outlets tuned to the 11am–4pm order 
rush
Key Metrics
Daily orders, attach rate, % of orders in peak window
Channels
Outlet visibility, peak-hour social content, word of 
mouth
Cost Structure
Outlet rent & staff, beans & milk, packaging
Revenue Streams
Per-cup sales + loyalty for repeat office customers

ASK YOURSELF FIRST
Which box exposes the real risk in your idea?
1 Which box on this canvas is hardest for your specific category, not categories in general?
2 For a physical product: is manufacturing the real constraint? For a service: is it your own bandwidth?
3 Would a customer with money already in hand actually behave the way your Channels box assumes?
