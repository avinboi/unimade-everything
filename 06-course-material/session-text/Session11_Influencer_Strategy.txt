KICKOFF  ·  5–12 MIN
Why Influencer Marketing Works, and Where It Doesn't
WHY IT WORKS
•   Borrowed trust — audiences trust a creator's word more than an 
ad
•   Cheaper storytelling — one relatable post often out-converts a 
produced ad
•   Reusable content — their video becomes your paid ad or website 
asset
•   Hyperlocal or niche reach an ad budget can't buy precisely
WHERE IT DOESN'T
•   Low-trust or regulated categories — a creator claim can create real 
liability
•   No visual or narrative hook (e.g. industrial B2B components)
•   Brand isn't ready to fulfill a demand spike (stockouts, weak ops)
•   One-off transactional purchase with no repeat value to justify the 
relationship

DEEP DIVE: FIT  ·  17–25 MIN
Which Categories Are Heavy on Influencers?
Before you build a strategy, be honest about whether your category even calls for one.
F&B, Beauty, Fashion, Lifestyle HIGH Visual, habitual purchase — trust transfers easily from a creator people already like
Wellness / Fitness / Personal Care HIGH Personal transformation stories drive genuine engagement, not just impressions
D2C Tech, Gadgets, Appliances MEDIUM Needs demo-capable creators (unboxing/review); works, but a slower sales cycle
B2B / Enterprise Services LOW–MED Credibility comes from operator/founder reach on LinkedIn, not lifestyle creators
Financial Products, Healthcare LOW Regulatory and trust risk — compliance-safe testimonials usually outperform influencer posts
Industrial / Commodity B2B LOW No visual or emotional hook — direct sales and case studies do more of the work

DEEP DIVE: ASK  ·  25–30 MIN
What Should You Actually Ask For?
GENERIC ASK
"Can you post about our brand?"
NARRATIVE-DRIVEN ASK
"We're trying to prove real people choose us over the chain café down the street for their daily order — could you film your actual 
1pm order at our outlet, no script, and just say what you'd tell a friend?"
The Ask should always answer: what STORY does this content prove, and to whom?

--- page 8 ---
DEEP DIVE: ASK  ·  30–35 MIN
Match Your Ask to Your Narrative
“We're the honest local alternative” Authenticity vs. bigger competitors Unscripted day-in-the-life or real-visit video
“This actually works” Product performance & credibility Before/after or side-by-side comparison
“Real people pay full price for this” Genuine demand, not a discount stunt Honest review + UGC reuse rights, no code
“We're for you, specifically” Community / niche relevance Co-created post in the community's own format
Founder or origin story Trust & differentiation Founder-led interview or “who's behind this” feature

DEEP DIVE: PROOF  ·  35–42 MIN
Choosing the Right KPI
Awareness / Reach Reach, saves, shares Ignore: The creator's follower count alone
Consideration / Trust Comment sentiment, click-throughs, DM inquiries Ignore: Like count in isolation
Conversion Promo code redemptions, affiliate sales, tracked orders Ignore: Engagement with no tracked code
Brand Equity / Community Repeat mentions, UGC reuse rights, sentiment over time Ignore: One-off post reach
If you can't name the KPI before you reach out, you're not ready to ask yet.

WORKED EXAMPLE  ·  46–50 MIN
Breww 47's Narrative-Driven Outreach
Narrative: “We're the honest local alternative to chain cafes for the 11am–4pm office rush.”
HOOK "Your reel about work-from-cafe setups this week was so real — the 1pm laptop-and-latte scramble is exactly our lunch rush."
FIT "We're Breww 47, a coffee outlet built around exactly that 11am–4pm office rush — your audience is our actual customer."
ASK "Would you be up for a free first order at your nearest outlet this week — no strings, just curious what you'd think?"
KPI for this one: does the visit convert into at least 3 tracked redemptions of a unique code. Total message length: 3 sentences.

TIERS & RATES  ·  50–55 MIN
Nano, Micro, Macro — and What Each Expects
Nano
1K–10K
Highest engagement, most affordable, feels 
like a real recommendation
Micro
10K–100K
The sweet spot for most small budgets — real 
reach, still personal
Macro
100K–1M
Broad reach, lower engagement rate, usually 
needs a real budget
SOUND REAL, NOT FAKE
Reference something specific and recent — a stock line about 'loving your 
content' is a giveaway.
RATE NEGOTIATION BASICS
Nano and micro creators often barter for product; macro usually expects a real 
rate card.
