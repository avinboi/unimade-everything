1. The one-page answer

Verdict

Yes, build Unimade as a student-life platform. Students already pay for food, laundry, housing, cleaning and storage, but they find these through word of mouth, notice boards and five WhatsApp groups that never reply. Partners (laundromats, tiffin kitchens, PG owners, cleaners, storage firms, brands) want students as customers but struggle to reach them. Unimade sits in the middle, makes it trusted and easy, and takes a cut. The business works if you do three things: (1) launch in one campus cluster, not everywhere; (2) add services in waves, each one earning its place; (3) make the money where it actually is, in the big moments like housing, move-in and brand deals, while everyday services keep students coming back.

2. What Unimade is

One sentence (Session 1 format)

"For hostel and PG students who are figuring out food, laundry, housing and everything else on their own, Unimade is a student-life platform that connects them to verified, student-priced providers in one place. Unlike scattered WhatsApp groups, notice boards and random vendors, everything on Unimade is checked, bookable in one chat, and backed by a Unimade guarantee."

Short version: "We help college students sort out everyday life by connecting them to verified providers near campus."

How it works

For a student

  1. Joins Unimade with a student ID (keeps it student-only)
  2. Sets hostel / PG and campus
  3. Messages Unimade or taps a service: "need a tiffin", "laundry pickup", "find me a PG under ₹12k"
  4. Gets 2–3 verified options at student prices, books, pays through Unimade
  5. If anything goes wrong, Unimade fixes it

For a partner

  1. Applies; Unimade checks quality, licences and prices ("Unimade Verified")
  2. Agrees a commission or fee and a student price
  3. Gets bookings from Unimade, delivers the service
  4. Gets paid by Unimade weekly, minus commission
  5. Can pay extra to be featured, or to run student campaigns

The important design choice: a branded marketplace

There are two ways to build a platform like this:

ModelHow it worksProblem
Open directory (like JustDial)List vendors, students contact them directly, vendors pay for leadsStudents meet the vendor once, then go direct forever. You lose the customer and the commission. No brand, no trust.
Branded marketplace (recommended)Students book and pay through Unimade. Unimade sets the standards, the student price and the guarantee. Partners deliver under "Unimade Verified".More work for you (support, payments, quality), but you own the customer relationship, which is the whole value of the business.

This is how the D2C course applies to a platform: the course is about owning the customer and the brand experience. Urban Company is the model to study. It doesn't employ every cleaner, but customers trust "Urban Company", not the individual provider. Unimade should be the same for student life.

Positioning

  • Tagline: keep "College life. Made easier." For ads and posters (S17 Single Minded Proposition, ≤5 words): "College life. Sorted."
  • Voice: your existing one, the funny older sibling ("Mom's not here. But we are." / "Same jeans since Monday? We noticed.").
  • Promise: "Verified. Student-priced. Sorted in one chat."

Why platforms win

  • Network effects: more students attract more partners; more partners mean better choice and prices, which attracts more students.
  • Light on assets: no kitchens, no washing machines, no buildings. Partners own those.
  • One customer, many sales: the student you win for tiffin also books laundry, a PG, storage. The cost of winning them is shared across every service.
  • Data: you learn what students need, when, and at what price. That's valuable to every brand that wants students.

4. The two customers

A platform has two sets of customers, and you have to win both.

Side 1: students (and their parents)

Early adopters: 1st and 2nd-year students living away from home in hostels and PGs in your Gurugram cluster. New to the city, no local network, short on time, on a budget of roughly ₹8,000–20,000 a month from parents estimate.

Parents: often the real payers for housing, tiffin and move-in. Worried about safety, food and hygiene. Their question is "is it verified?", and "Unimade Verified" answers it.

Size of the first market: about 5 campuses and their PGs, roughly 3,000–5,000 students. The year-1 plan reaches 3,500 verified students.

Side 2: partners

  • Laundromats and laundry brands near campus
  • Tiffin kitchens, cloud kitchens, nearby cafés
  • PG owners and student-housing operators
  • Cleaners, electricians, plumbers, phone and laptop repair shops
  • Storage companies and packers/movers
  • Clinics, pharmacies, gyms
  • Brands that want students (food, personal care, apps, banks, edtech)
  • Employers offering internships and part-time work

What Unimade should not do

  • On-demand food delivery (one meal at a time): Swiggy and Zomato do this with fleets you can't match. Focus on monthly tiffin plans and café deals, which they don't do well for students.
  • Cook, wash or clean yourself. Partners deliver; Unimade connects, checks and guarantees.
  • Random errands: every errand is different, so they can't be priced or quality-checked. Leave them out until much later.

The key insight: daily services bring students back, big moments make the money

Laundry, food and cleaning together are about 28% of what Unimade keeps, even though they're most of the orders. PG placements, the Move-in Kit and storage make about 53%. Brand and partner fees make about 19%. So everyday services are your engagement engine: they keep students opening Unimade every week, so that when the big moment comes (a new PG, moving out, freshers' season), Unimade is the obvious first stop. Don't judge laundry by its ₹46 per order. Judge it by how many students it keeps active.

Your earlier pricing, revisited

Your newsletter priced each order at a ₹50 service fee + ₹30 delivery, with the partner commission on top. On a ₹200 laundry order, that's 40% extra for the student, and students would go straight to the vendor to avoid it (leakage). The fix:

  • No student fees at launch. The partner pays the commission; the student sees one clean price that's equal to or lower than going direct (partners give a student price in exchange for volume).
  • Delivery or pickup costs are built into the partner's price, not added on.
  • Unimade+ (₹149/month) comes in year 2, only once students use three or more services and there are real perks to sell.

The most common platform mistake: confusing GMV with revenue

If ₹59,06,780 of bookings go through Unimade, it's tempting to say "we did ₹59 lakh". But Unimade's revenue is ₹19,89,586; the rest belongs to partners. Report both, and never mix them up, especially with investors or in competitions.

The PG placement maths (your most valuable service)

  • Average PG rent near campus ≈ ₹12,000/month estimate. Owners often pay brokers to fill beds; Unimade asks for 50% of one month's rent, payable when the student moves in.
  • One placement leaves ≈ ₹3,800 after paying the ambassador who helped and allowing for owners who don't pay.
  • Just 8 placements a month cover ₹30,000 of fixed costs. The plan has 102 placements in year 1, mostly June–August.
  • For students it's free, and safer than a broker: verified photos, a checklist visit, and Unimade stepping in if the PG doesn't match what was promised.

Leakage: what it costs

If 40% of laundry students went direct to the laundromat after a few months, that's ≈ ₹11,830 a month lost at October volumes, plus the lost chance to sell them other services. Section 12 covers how to prevent it.

Move-in Kit and ads

The kit's gross margin is 66.4% (passes the course's 65% gate), so its break-even ROAS is 1.51 by the course formula and 1.93 with all costs counted. Spend up to ₹1,384 to win a kit sale and you still break even; target ₹500–700.

The rule for adding a service (the "service gate")

A new service goes live only when all five are true. This is the platform version of the course's 65% margin gate: a check you pass before spending.

  1. Demand: at least 30 students have asked for it (in interviews, WhatsApp or the community).
  2. Supply: at least 2 partners are signed, verified and tested (you or a friend tried them).
  3. Money: commission or fee agreed in writing, and Unimade keeps at least ₹40 per transaction after costs.
  4. Quality: a written standard (time, price, hygiene, what happens if it goes wrong).
  5. Capacity: existing services are running at ≥ 90% on-time and ≥ 4.3/5 rating. Don't add a new service while the old ones are shaky.

The freshers' bundle (Wave 3's big idea)

One offer for new students and their parents, sold from May: "Moving to college? Unimade sorts it."

  • Verified PG shortlist + visit with an ambassador (free for the student)
  • Move-in Kit delivered to the PG on arrival day (₹2,999)
  • First week of tiffin at a discount, first laundry pickup free
  • Access to the Unimade community of their campus

Parents get peace of mind; you get a student on day one who uses every service for the next 3–4 years.

"Unimade Verified": the standard

  • Documents: ID; FSSAI registration for food; ownership or lease proof for PGs; GST if they have it.

Student pays Unimade → Unimade keeps its commission → pays the partner weekly. Razorpay's split-payment product ("Route") can pay partners automatically, taking your share at source. PG success fees are invoiced to the owner on move-in day; collect a token upfront if possible.

  1. Morning: check new WhatsApp requests; send options within 1 hour (liquidity!).
  2. Confirm bookings with partners; send payment links.

The Unimade guarantee

"If something booked on Unimade goes wrong, we fix it or refund it." Caps: laundry items up to ₹1,000 per order; tiffin, a free replacement meal or a refund for the day; PG, help finding another verified PG free, and the owner's fee withheld. Fund it with a reserve of about 2% of GMV, and recover from partners where it was their fault.

14. Legal, money and team

General guidance, not legal or tax advice. A platform that collects money for other businesses has extra tax rules, so book a session with a chartered accountant (CA) before you start collecting payments for partners.

StageStructure
Pilot (Nov–Dec)Separate bank account; keep every record. Keep volumes small.

GST: why platforms are different

  • A platform that collects payments for others can be treated as an "e-commerce operator" under GST. E-commerce operators may need to register regardless of turnover and collect TCS (tax at source) on partners' sales.
  • For some services (restaurant/food services, and housekeeping such as cleaning and repairs when the provider isn't GST-registered), the law can make the platform itself liable to pay GST on the service.
  • Your own commissions and fees are a service; GST applies once you cross the ₹20 lakh threshold (or earlier if registered).
  • The Move-in Kit: selling on Amazon/Flipkart needs GST from the first sale (S16).
  • Practical approach: in the pilot, consider letting students pay partners directly for food and home services while Unimade invoices partners for commission, until a CA confirms the right setup. Ask specifically about Section 9(5) and TCS.

The team

The website lists five people; one is working. A platform has two sides, field work, support and partner sales, so it needs at least two committed people. Fix this first:

  1. One honest conversation with each person: in or out, with hours per week, for 6 months.
  2. Equity follows future work, not past attendance; people stepping back get a small thank-you share or none, in writing.
  3. Vesting: shares earned over 4 years with a 1-year cliff.
  4. A one-page founders' agreement now; formalise it when you register.
  5. Update the website to show the real team.

Suggested roles: Founder 1 (you): partners, operations, numbers. Founder 2: students, brand, content, community. Then campus ambassadors (commission-based), a part-time ops associate from January, and a PG field executive from summer.

18. Things you didn't ask but need to know

  1. Trust is the product. Students can already find a tiffin or a PG. What they can't find is one they can trust. Every decision should make Unimade more trustworthy.
  2. Supply first, in a tiny area. Ten great partners in one cluster beat a hundred average ones across Gurugram.
  3. Speed of reply is your service. In a WhatsApp-run platform, a 5-minute reply feels like magic; a 5-hour reply feels like the old WhatsApp groups you're replacing.
  4. Registered students are a vanity number. Count active students and services per student.
  5. Your community is a moat. A student-only, campus-only community (buy/sell, questions, events) gives students a reason to open Unimade even when they don't need to book anything.
  6. Brands will pay for students, but only once you have proof. Keep data on how many students saw, tried and used something. That's what a brand buys.
  7. Never sell student data. Sell reach (sampling, posters, offers), never phone numbers. Breaking that trust ends the business, and the DPDP Act forbids it.
  8. Each service needs an owner. Even if it's an ambassador, someone must be responsible for each live service's partners and ratings.
  9. Write the playbook as you go. The value of Unimade at the end of year one is the playbook that lets you open cluster #2 in 6 weeks.
  10. Your course work and your business are the same. Use real Unimade numbers in every course deliverable.
  11. Say "not yet", not "no". Write down every service students ask for; that list becomes Waves 4, 5 and 6.
  12. Keep fixed costs near zero until revenue is real. Hire after the work exists, not before.

Appendix B: Student interview script (15 minutes)

  1. What were the hardest things about moving into your hostel / PG? (Let them list; don't suggest.)
  2. Rank these by how much they bother you: food, laundry, finding a PG, cleaning, repairs, storage, health, money.
  3. For your top 3: what do you do today? What does it cost you a month? When did it last go wrong?
  4. How did you find your current PG / tiffin / laundry person? Did you pay a broker?
  5. Who pays for these: you or your parents?
  6. Now describe Unimade. Which service would you use first? What would make you not trust it?
  7. Can I add you to the launch list? (Phone number = the only answer that counts.)
